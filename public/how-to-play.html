Enclave is a one-rule puzzle played on a 9×9 board against a draining clock. Every board below is live: click a cell to drop the piece and watch the rule work.

01

The one rule

Drag pieces onto the board. When a patch of empty cells is completely surrounded by blocks, you claim it: the room and its walls vanish, and you score the room's area squared, times ten. This 2×2 room is missing one wall. The gold cell is the game's own hint: one block there closes it.

Three things happened. The four empty cells scored 4² × 10 = 160. The eight fence blocks were removed, which is how you keep the board from filling up. And the clock gained time: 1.8 s for placing plus 2.0 + 0.8 × 4 = 5.2 s for the claim, so 7.0 s if you moved fast (more on speed in step 7).

Walls only need to touch the room on its four sides. The diagonal corners are not part of the fence, they stay on the board, and you never need to build them.

02

The edge is not a wall

Empty cells that touch the board edge are always "outside", no matter how many blocks surround them. A room needs blocks on all sides, so anything hugging the edge can never be claimed. Build your fence at least one cell in.

03

Points: area squared

Squaring is the whole strategy. Four separate 1-cell rooms are worth 40 points. One 2×2 room is worth 160. A 3×3 is 810, and it takes only 12 blocks of fence. Pick a room size to see the maths the game runs on every claim.

Sixteen blocks spent on four 1×1 rooms score 40. The same sixteen blocks around one 4×4 room score 2,560. Rooms of 9 cells or more get the BIG ROOM label; 16 or more, MASSIVE ROOM.

04

Two rooms, one piece

When a single placement closes several rooms at once, the whole claim is multiplied by 1 + 0.5 × (rooms − 1): ×1.5 for two rooms, ×2 for three. Here two 2×2 rooms each have a gap, and the gaps sit side by side. Both are gold. Lay the DOMINO across both.

05

Streaks

Claims on consecutive placements build a streak, and each claim is multiplied by 1 + 0.25 × streak, capped at ×3. The streak counts the claims before this one, so the first claim is ×1, the second ×1.25, the third ×1.5, and the cap arrives on the ninth. In Classic the streak survives two non-claiming placements and dies on the third; in Blitz it dies on the second. The green pips count down to that reset.

Shared walls

07

The clock

The time bank drains continuously and only placements refill it. Every placement adds time, every claim adds more (bigger rooms add more, up to a cap), and the whole bonus is scaled by how fast you moved: 1.0× immediately after your last placement, sliding down to 0.45× over 8 seconds in Classic. Hesitating is expensive. The drain itself also speeds up the longer you survive.

Two ways to lose. TIME'S UP when the bank hits zero. NO ROOM LEFT when the piece in hand fits nowhere in any rotation and swapping with the hold slot cannot rescue you. Claiming rooms clears space, so a player who keeps closing rooms rarely sees the second one.

08

Controls and signals

Controls

  • Drag the piece onto the board. A green ghost means the drop is valid, red means it is not. The piece floats above your finger so your thumb never hides it.
  • Rotate: tap the piece, tap ROTATE, or press R. Every piece rotates clockwise; fence-building is about orientation.
  • Hold: tap the HOLD slot or press H to park the piece for later and take the next one. Once per piece.
  • Next: two upcoming pieces are always visible. Plan which gap each one closes.
  • Esc or P pauses. The game also pauses itself when you switch tabs.

Pieces

Pieces are dealt from a shuffled bag so straight lines and corners, the fence material, always come back around. Per bag: DOT ×1, DOMINO ×3, BAR 3 ×3, CORNER ×3, BAR 4 ×2, L ×2, J ×2, and one each of T, S, Z, SQUARE, BAR 5, BIG L and U.

09

How to score more

  1. Build one big room, not many small ones. Area squared means a 3×3 (810) beats twenty 1×1s (200) and costs fewer blocks.
  2. Skip the corners. Only the cells directly beside the room are the fence. Corner stones are wasted blocks.
  3. Stay off the edge. Rooms touching the border never close. Fence one cell in and use the edge row for your walls.
  4. Line up the gaps. Leave the last gaps of two rooms next to each other and close both with one piece for ×1.5.
  5. Keep the streak alive. One claim every three placements keeps the multiplier growing; nine claims in a row reach the ×3 cap.
  6. Move fast. The same placement earns 1.8 s now and 0.8 s after eight seconds of thinking. Decide while the piece is still in the queue.
  7. Hold the closer. If the perfect piece arrives early, park it in HOLD and finish the fence first.
  8. Close before you flood. Every claim removes its fence. If the board tints orange, cash in a room to make space.

The 60-second demo

Showing the game to someone? This order teaches the rule, the payoff and the pressure in a minute.

  1. Menu. Point at the logo assembling itself around a gold room. Pick Classic, press PLAY, dismiss the three-step tutorial.
  2. Controls. Tap the piece to rotate it. Tap the HOLD slot to park it.
  3. First claim. Build a small ring with two corners and two bars, leaving one gap. Point at the gold cell. Drop the closer: a 2×2 room, "+160", sparkles, a shockwave, and time landing on the clock.
  4. Big room. Build a 12-block ring for a 3×3 room: "+810", the BIG ROOM label, a harder shake and a flash.
  5. Double close. Set up two small rooms with their gaps on the same row and close both with one bar: "2 ROOMS ×1.5".
  6. Pressure. Let the clock fall under 10 seconds: red glow, muffled music, urgent ticks. Then the game-over screen with stats, the leaderboard name entry and SHARE.
