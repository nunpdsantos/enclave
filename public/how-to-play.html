Enclave · how to play

Fence it in.
Claim the room.
Beat the clock.

Enclave is a one-rule puzzle played on a 9×9 board — against a draining clock in Classic and Blitz, against a ration of thirty pieces in the Daily. Every board below is live: click a cell to drop the piece and watch the rule work.

01

The one rule

Drag pieces onto the board. When a patch of empty cells is completely surrounded by blocks, you claim it: the room and its walls vanish, and you score the room's area squared, times ten. This 2×2 room is missing one wall. The gold cell is the game's own hint: one block there closes it.

Three things happened. The four empty cells scored 4² × 10 = 160. The eight fence blocks were removed, which is how you keep the board from filling up. And the clock gained time: 1.8 s for placing plus 2.0 + 0.8 × 4 = 5.2 s for the claim, so 7.0 s if you moved fast (more on speed in step 8).

Walls only need to touch the room on its four sides. The diagonal corners are not part of the fence, they stay on the board, and you never need to build them.

02

The edge is not a wall

Empty cells that touch the board edge are always "outside", no matter how many blocks surround them. A room needs blocks on all sides, so anything hugging the edge can never be claimed. Build your fence at least one cell in.

03

Squaring is the whole strategy. Four separate 1-cell rooms are worth 40 points. One 2×2 room is worth 160. A 3×3 is 810, and it takes only 12 blocks of fence. Pick a room size to see the maths the game runs on every claim.

04

Territory and the Survey

The board remembers. Every cell you claim stays lit for the rest of the run, and a room built on lit floor pays less than the same room on new ground. A claim is scaled by how much of its floor is fresh:

factor = 0.5 + 0.5 × fresh ÷ area

All-new ground gives 1.0 and pays the full area² × 10. Floor that has all been claimed before gives 0.5. A mix lands in between, pro rata.

So claiming the same corner over and over gets steadily less profitable, and the run pushes you outward. When a claim pays less than full price the board says so: RELIT ×0.5 next to the score popup. The streak demo in step 6 lights its floor as you claim and reports the factor on every claim.

The Survey

Only the inner 7×7 can ever be floor. The board edge is never a wall, so an edge cell can never be inside a room, which leaves 49 cells that can be lit. Light all of them and you complete a Survey: a flat bonus paid on top of the claim and multiplied by nothing — not by the streak, not by a double close. The map then goes dark and the next survey starts from an empty one, which is what gives a long run an arc instead of the same claim repeated.

06

Streaks

Claims on consecutive placements build a streak, and each claim is multiplied by 1 + 0.25 × streak, capped at ×3. The streak counts the claims before this one, so the first claim is ×1, the second ×1.25, the third ×1.5, and the cap arrives on the ninth. In Classic the streak survives two non-claiming placements and dies on the third; in Blitz it dies on the second. The green pips count down to that reset.

07

Echo walls

Claiming a room removes every block in its fence, including a wall it shares with the room next door — which on its own would make neighbours a punishment. So the wall does not go all at once. For 2.0 seconds in Classic, 1.5 in Blitz, every block a claim removed stays on the board as a fading ghost that still counts as a wall, and a room closed against one pays ×1.25 with an ECHO label.

That turns the shared wall from a pitfall into the game's signature combo: claim the left room, then close the right one before its borrowed wall fades. Miss the window and the ghost is gone, and the survivor needs the wall rebuilt.

While the ghosts stand they count as walls, so the board lights up with cells that would close something off them. Every one of those goes out when the echo fades.

The floor the claim just took is held solid for the same window, and it is never drawn. That is the one thing echoes must not allow: a room re-closing itself off its own ghosts. The ghost walls you can see fading; the floor under them you cannot, so nothing invisible can pay you.

No echoes in the Daily. The window is measured in seconds and the Daily has no clock, so it would only reward whoever happened to play fast — the one thing that mode is not about.

08

The clock

Classic and Blitz are timed; the Daily is not (step 9). The time bank drains continuously and only placements refill it. Every placement adds time, every claim adds more (bigger rooms add more, up to a cap), and the whole bonus is scaled by how fast you moved: 1.0× immediately after your last placement, sliding down to 0.45× over 8 seconds in Classic. Hesitating is expensive. The drain itself also speeds up the longer you survive.

Two ways to lose. TIME'S UP when the bank hits zero. NO ROOM LEFT when the piece in hand fits nowhere in any rotation and swapping with the hold slot cannot rescue you. Claiming rooms clears space, so a player who keeps closing rooms rarely sees the second one.

09

The Rationed Daily

One puzzle a day, the same one for everyone: 30 pieces, no clock. The seed is the UTC date, so two players anywhere in the world are dealt the same shapes, in the same rotations, in the same colours. Scarcity replaces time as the pressure and the whole game becomes planning.

  • Thirty placements, and that is the run. The HUD counts them down in the clock's place — PIECES 17/30 — and turns gold with five left. Place the last one and the run ends as a completion rather than a death: RATION SPENT, and the score counts in full.
  • No clock at all. Nothing drains, placements add nothing, and there is no speed bonus, so there is no reward for hurrying and no penalty for thinking.
  • HOLD works as it always does, once per piece. The one refusal is parking the last piece of the ration with an empty hold slot, which would leave your hand with nothing to place.
  • First submission counts. Play it again as often as you like; later runs are labelled PRACTICE RUN · NOT SUBMITTED. The board keeps the score you posted first, higher or not, so it measures the puzzle and not how many attempts you had.
  • No echo walls and no tier bag. An echo window is measured in seconds and there is no clock to measure them against; and a bag that tightens with the score would deal two players different pieces on the same puzzle.
  • The day rolls over at UTC midnight, not yours, so everyone is on the same puzzle at the same instant. The menu shows RESETS IN hh:mm, and the personal best is per day — a lifetime daily best would only say you once had a good seed.
  • A survey pays 2,000, and the streak survives two placements without a claim, as in Classic.

The Daily has its own tier ladder, lower than Classic's, in the table in step 11.

10

Controls and signals

Controls

  • Drag the piece onto the board. A green ghost means the drop is valid, red means it is not. The piece floats above your finger so your thumb never hides it.
  • Rotate: tap the piece, tap ROTATE, or press R. Every piece rotates clockwise; fence-building is about orientation.
  • Hold: tap the HOLD slot or press H to park the piece for later and take the next one. Once per piece.
  • Next: two upcoming pieces are always visible. Plan which gap each one closes.
  • Esc or P pauses. The game also pauses itself when you switch tabs.

Pieces

Pieces are dealt from a shuffled bag so straight lines and corners, the fence material, always come back around. Per bag: DOT ×1, DOMINO ×3, BAR 3 ×3, CORNER ×3, BAR 4 ×2, L ×2, J ×2, and one each of T, S, Z, SQUARE, BAR 5, BIG L and U.

The bag by tier

From ARCHITECT upward the bag gets stingier with fence material. Difficulty here is the mix, not the pace: every bag still holds 23 or 24 pieces, so the deal keeps its rhythm and only the material changes. The new mix lands at the next refill, never mid-bag, and the tier card says TOUGHER BAG when it is coming.

Options

Three pills on the menu turn SOUND, MUSIC and HAPTIC on and off, and OPTIONS holds the rest. SFX and MUSIC sliders start below full, so the mix the game shipped with is the one you start from and there is somewhere to go louder. MOTION is SYSTEM, REDUCED or FULL: SYSTEM follows your device's reduced-motion setting, and REDUCED drops screen shake, flashes, zoom pulses and slow-motion and keeps a quarter of the particles — claim outlines, dissolves and score popups stay, because they say what just happened. COLOURS switches the pieces to a colour-blind-safe palette that survives deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia. LAYOUT mirrors HOLD and NEXT for left-thumb play, from the next run. Every row applies on the spot and is remembered.

11

How to score more

  1. Build one big room, not many small ones. Area squared means a 3×3 (810) beats twenty 1×1s (200) and costs fewer blocks.
  2. Skip the corners. Only the cells directly beside the room are the fence. Corner stones are wasted blocks.
  3. Stay off the edge. Rooms touching the border never close. Fence one cell in and use the edge row for your walls.
  4. Line up the gaps. Leave the last gaps of two rooms next to each other and close both with one piece for ×1.5.
  5. Keep the streak alive. One claim every three placements keeps the multiplier growing; nine claims in a row reach the ×3 cap.
  6. Move fast. The same placement earns 1.8 s now and 0.8 s after eight seconds of thinking. Decide while the piece is still in the queue.
  7. Hold the closer. If the perfect piece arrives early, park it in HOLD and finish the fence first.
  8. Close before you flood. Every claim removes its fence. If the board tints orange, cash in a room to make space.
  9. Chase the echo. After a claim you have about two seconds where its fence is still a wall. The room next door, closed inside that window, pays ×1.25 — and it is the cheapest multiplier in the game because the wall is already built.
  10. Move onto new ground. Relit floor pays half, so the same corner gets worse every time. Spreading out is also the only way to a survey, which is worth more than any single room.

After the run

The game-over screen carries insights: at most three lines about the run you just played, ordered most useful first, so points you could have had and know how to get next time come before a description of what happened, and praise comes last. Under the leaderboard on the menu, stats keeps the lifetime totals for each mode — runs, best, biggest room, rooms sealed, surveys, the room size you actually build most often, time played, last played. Every run folds in exactly once, quits included.

SHARE builds a 1080×1350 card with the score, the mode, your rooms, biggest room, streak, tier and surveys, and the territory map the run finished on. It goes out as an image where the browser will take one, as text where it will not, and to the clipboard where it will do neither.

12

The 60-second demo

Showing the game to someone? This order teaches the rule, the payoff and the pressure in a minute.

  1. Menu. Point at the logo assembling itself around a gold room. Pick Classic, press PLAY, dismiss the three-step tutorial.
  2. Controls. Tap the piece to rotate it. Tap the HOLD slot to park it.
  3. First claim. Build a small ring with two corners and two bars, leaving one gap. Point at the gold cell. Drop the closer: a 2×2 room, "+160", sparkles, a shockwave, and time landing on the clock.
  4. Big room. Build a 12-block ring for a 3×3 room: "+810", the BIG ROOM label, a harder shake and a flash.
  5. Double close. Set up two small rooms with their gaps on the same row and close both with one bar: "2 ROOMS ×1.5".
  6. Echo. Build two rooms sharing a wall, close one, and close the other while the shared wall is still fading: "ECHO ×1.25". This is the trick the game is actually about.
  7. Pressure. Let the clock fall under 10 seconds: red glow, muffled music, urgent ticks. Then the game-over screen with stats, the leaderboard name entry and SHARE.
